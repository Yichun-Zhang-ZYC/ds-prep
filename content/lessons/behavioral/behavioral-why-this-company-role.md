## Why it matters
Interviewers use "Why us?" to test genuine interest, preparation and whether your goals fit what the job offers. Generic praise ("great culture, great brand") is the most common way to lose points.

## Concept
**Three pillars (company, role, you)**:
1. **Company**: a specific, researched reason: product, mission, a technical problem they publish about, a recent launch or strategic move, or their data scale. Show you used the product.
2. **Role**: what you will do day to day and why it fits (the stack, the problem type, the stakeholders, the impact surface).
3. **You**: how your experience prepares you and what you will grow into; link to a thread in your career.

**Research checklist** (30-60 minutes): product (use it and note one improvement idea), recent news and earnings or funding, engineering/data blog posts and papers, the job description keywords, the team and interviewers' backgrounds, competitors and the business model.

**Structure**: 1 sentence on what attracts you (specific), 1-2 sentences connecting your skills to the role's problems, 1 sentence on the growth you want. About 45-60 seconds.

**Avoid**: compensation or brand-only reasons, criticising your current employer, flattery that would apply to any company, or reasons that suggest you want a different job ("I want to move to engineering" when applying for analytics).

**Related**: "Why are you leaving?" Answer with a pull toward the new opportunity, not a push away from the old one; stay factual and positive.

## Worked example
**Weak answer**: "I've always admired your brand, the culture looks great, and it's a fast-growing company." This could be said about any employer.

**Strong answer** (data scientist applying to a grocery-delivery company's substitutions team, about 50 seconds):

"Two things drew me in. First, I use the app weekly and noticed that when an item is out of stock, the replacement is often a different size or brand; your engineering blog post on substitution ranking says that accounts for a large share of support contacts, and it's a clean ranking-plus-personalisation problem. Second, the data is rich: millions of accept/reject decisions a week give you a feedback signal most recommender problems lack.

On my side, I've built ranking models at a retailer and ran the A/B tests that took substitution acceptance from 61% to 66%, so I can contribute from week one on offline evaluation and experimentation. I'd like to grow into real-time personalisation at your scale, which I can't practise where I am."

Anatomy: specific hook (product + blog), problem fit, proof with numbers (+5 points = $66 - 61$), and a growth reason that only this company can satisfy.

> [!TIP] Interview tips
> - Prepare a three-part answer (company, role, you) and keep a different specific hook for each company.
> - Use the product before the interview and bring one observation or improvement idea.
> - Pitfall: answers that fit any employer. If it works for another company, make it more specific.
