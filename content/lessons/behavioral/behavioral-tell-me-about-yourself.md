## Why it matters
It is the first question in almost every interview and sets the narrative for everything after. A tight 90-second pitch that connects your past to *this* role beats a chronological resume recital.

## Concept
**Present, Past, Future** (about 90 seconds, 200-250 words):
1. **Present**: your current role and a headline strength, with one proof point ("I'm a data scientist on the pricing team, where I built the demand model that lifted margin 2%").
2. **Past**: 1-2 earlier experiences that explain how you got here and add relevant skills (not a full chronology).
3. **Future**: why this role, linking what you want to do next with what the team needs.

**Tailor** the headline and proof points to the job description: for a product analytics role stress experimentation and stakeholder impact; for an ML engineering role stress productionisation and scale. Pick the 2-3 most relevant themes and drop the rest.

**Rules**: lead with strengths and impact, include 1-2 numbers, keep the tone conversational, avoid personal details with no relevance, and never recite your resume line by line. End with a hook that invites a follow-up question ("which I'd love to talk about more").

**Variants**: "Walk me through your resume" lets you go chronological but still spend 70% on the last 1-2 roles; "Tell me about yourself" for a recruiter should be shorter (60 seconds).

## Worked example
**Model answer** (data scientist applying to a marketplace analytics team, about 55 seconds):

"I'm a data scientist at a retail company, where for the last three years I've owned experimentation and pricing analytics. Most recently I built a demand-elasticity model and ran the A/B tests behind it; the resulting price changes lifted gross margin by 2%, around $3M a year.

Before that I started as an analyst in supply chain, where I learned SQL deeply and automated a weekly forecasting report, saving the team about 15 hours a week. That is where I realised I enjoy turning messy data into decisions that people actually use.

What draws me to your team is the marketplace setting: two-sided pricing and experiments with network effects are problems I haven't solved yet but have been preparing for through interference-aware testing. I'd like to bring my experimentation and pricing background to that."

Structure check: Present (with a number), Past and Future each take about a third. About 135 words, or roughly 55 seconds at 150 words per minute; add a second proof point to fill 90 seconds.

> [!TIP] Interview tips
> - Write the script, time it, and cut it to 90 seconds; then practise until it sounds natural, not memorised.
> - Always include one quantified accomplishment and one sentence on why this role.
> - Pitfall: a resume monologue. Pick the 2-3 themes the job description cares about.
