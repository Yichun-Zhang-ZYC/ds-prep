## Why it matters
The last five minutes are a signal and a decision tool: good questions show curiosity and judgement, and help you learn whether the job is one you want. "No questions" reads as low interest.

## Concept
**Categories**: (1) **Role and success**: what the first 90 days and first year look like; how success is measured; (2) **Team and process**: how data science works with product and engineering; how decisions are made; how experiments are reviewed; (3) **Data and tech**: data quality and ownership, tooling, how models reach production, tech debt; (4) **Business and strategy**: the top priorities this year and how the team contributes; (5) **Growth and culture**: feedback, mentoring, career paths, how disagreement is handled; (6) **Closing**: concerns about my fit, and the next steps.

**Tailor to the interviewer**: hiring manager (expectations, team direction, management style), peer (day-to-day, on-call, code review, pain points), senior leader (strategy, where data fits), recruiter (process, timeline, team structure).

**Quality bar**: open-ended, specific, shows you did the research, and listens to what was said earlier; ask a follow-up to their answer. Prepare 8-10 and pick 3-5 live.

**Avoid**: anything answered on the website, compensation, perks or leave in early rounds (save for the recruiter or offer), criticism ("Why is your product bad?"), and yes/no questions.

**Useful closers**: "What would make someone exceptional in this role?" and "Do you have any hesitations about my background that I can address?"

## Worked example
**Scenario**: final round with the hiring manager of a product-data-science team; 5 minutes left. Five questions, with what each achieves:

1. "What would success look like in the first 90 days, and how would you measure it?" Reveals expectations, and lets you restate them to confirm fit.
2. "Can you tell me about a recent experiment that surprised the team, and what you did with it?" Tests experimentation culture and data-driven decision making.
3. "How do models or analyses typically go from a notebook to something the business uses?" Exposes production maturity and where you would add value.
4. "What's the hardest part of the role that the job description doesn't say?" Invites honesty about pain points, a good sign when answered openly.
5. "Is there anything about my background that gives you pause, so I can address it now?" Surfaces objections while you can still answer.

Listen for red flags: vague answers on metrics, no process for data quality, high turnover ("the last two people left within a year"), or no clear owner for the team's roadmap. Take notes and send a thank-you email the same day that mentions one specific part of the conversation.

> [!TIP] Interview tips
> - Prepare 8-10 questions across role, team, data, business and growth; ask 3-5 live and follow up on the answers.
> - End with 'Is there anything about my background that gives you pause?' to surface and address concerns.
> - Pitfall: asking about compensation first, or asking nothing at all.
